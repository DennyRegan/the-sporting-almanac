Three days after Marta’s extraordinary display against the United States, Brazil reached the match they had never played before: a Women’s World Cup final. Germany were waiting in Shanghai on 30 September 2007. They were the defending champions, and through five games in China they had not conceded a goal. Brazil had the tournament’s most arresting attacker. The final would ask whether one of those records had to give way.

Germany’s defence had been reliable from the opening day, when the team beat Argentina 11–0. Nadine Angerer, their goalkeeper, had completed every match without retrieving the ball from her net. Brazil’s route looked different. Formiga and Cristiane supported Marta’s movement and improvisation; the 4–0 semi-final win over the US had shown how far that combination could take them. In Shanghai, Brazil made chances and kept the pressure on Germany, but could not turn it into a lead.

Birgit Prinz scored in the 52nd minute. Her finish put Germany ahead without settling the contest. Soon afterwards Cristiane was brought down in the area, giving Marta a penalty and Brazil a direct chance to erase the deficit. Marta struck low; Angerer dived to her right and saved. The moment brought the two central figures of the final face to face, and the goalkeeper won it.

Brazil pressed again, but Germany did not concede. Simone Laudehr headed in a second goal with four minutes remaining. Germany won 2–0 and became the first team to retain the Women’s World Cup. Angerer had gone through the entire tournament without conceding, 540 minutes in all. Her penalty save was the most visible act in a defensive record built across six matches.

The result gave Brazil a painful ending to the best Women’s World Cup run in their history. Marta had scored seven goals at the tournament and would collect both the Golden Boot and Golden Ball. Neither award could alter the final. She later spoke of chances Brazil had failed to take, including the penalty, while recognising how much reaching the match had meant to her team.

Marta’s semi-final had ended with her picking a route past defenders to score. In the final, her clearest route to goal was a penalty. Angerer read it, reached it and kept Germany’s net untouched to the end. Prinz and Laudehr scored the goals that won the title; that save was the instant in which Brazil’s best chance to take it from them disappeared.

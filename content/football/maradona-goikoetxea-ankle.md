Barcelona beat Athletic Club 4–0 on 24 September 1983. The four goals are there in both clubs’ records. The image that endured was Diego Maradona leaving the Camp Nou on a stretcher.

Athletic had won the Spanish league the previous season. Barcelona, coached by César Luis Menotti, had reason to think this could be their year. Maradona and Bernd Schuster had begun to build a partnership; in June, Barça had beaten Real Madrid in both the Copa del Rey and the Copa de la Liga. During the latter tie, a Maradona goal at the Bernabéu had drawn applause from the home supporters. His talent was obvious, even if his first season in Spain had been broken up by hepatitis.

The fixture fell on La Mercè, Barcelona’s festival day. By half-time, goals from Periko Alonso and Julio Alberto had put the home side two up. The second campaign was only four league matches old when Andoni Goikoetxea’s challenge brought Maradona down as he moved to control a ball in midfield. The next day’s report in *El País* described him being carried off and the anger in the ground. Barcelona’s account of the medical finding is specific: a fracture around the left ankle, with ligament damage.

Goikoetxea was already a difficult name for Barcelona to hear. In December 1981, his challenge on Schuster had caused a serious knee injury that kept the German out for a long spell. To Barça supporters watching Maradona depart, this was a second damaging collision between the Athletic defender and one of their most important players. The fact that Barcelona went on to score twice more, through Marcos Alonso and Lobo Carrasco, did not repair what had happened.

Maradona returned in January 1984 and scored twice against Sevilla in his comeback. That recovery deserves its place in the story: the September injury curtailed his season, but it did not end it. Barcelona nevertheless finished behind Athletic, who retained the league and then beat Barça in the Copa del Rey final. That final ended with a fight involving players from both sides. By the summer Maradona’s relationship with Barcelona’s board had deteriorated beyond repair, and he left for Napoli.

It would be too neat to claim that one tackle alone sent him to Italy. Illness, disagreements at the club and the violence after the cup final all belong in the account. Yet the sequence began to turn sharply on this September evening. A home win over the champions should have been evidence that Barcelona could take the league from them. Instead, their best player spent the next three months recovering from a broken ankle. At Napoli, he would go on to win two Serie A titles and the UEFA Cup.

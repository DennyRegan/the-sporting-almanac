The New York Cosmos landed in Calcutta with the billing. Mohun Bagan supplied the contest. On 24 September 1977, Pelé played at Eden Gardens in one of his last appearances before retirement. The score, less widely recalled outside India, was 2–2.

The visit was the work of Mohun Bagan official Dhiren De. Pelé had arrived two nights earlier with a side that also included Carlos Alberto, captain of Brazil’s 1970 World Cup winners, and the Italian forward Giorgio Chinaglia. The Cosmos had won the North American Soccer League title in August and were nearing the end of a tour of Asia. A week after Calcutta, Pelé would play his farewell match against Santos in New Jersey.

Bagan’s players understood the occasion. Their captain, Subrata Bhattacharya, later told the All India Football Federation that some of them stopped warming up to watch Pelé. Yet the wet ground at Eden Gardens, battered by monsoon rain, still had to be played on; the visiting names conferred no advantage on the scorecard.

There was a local season at stake as well. Bagan had recently lost a Calcutta League derby to East Bengal, and the clubs had another meeting ahead in the IFA Shield. Chowdhury later wrote that a heavy defeat to Cosmos would have done little for the squad’s confidence. For the hosts, the afternoon was more than a chance to collect autographs.

Carlos Alberto put Cosmos ahead. Shyam Thapa equalised and Mohammed Habib put Bagan in front, turning an exhibition conceived around Pelé into a lead for the local club. Cosmos recovered through a penalty that Bagan defender Pradip Chowdhury, writing years later about the match, remembered as contentious. His account is useful for more than the grievance: it describes the tactical assignment behind the result. Chowdhury and Gautam Sarkar were asked to contain Pelé, with Sarkar following him higher up the pitch.

They could hardly remove him from the game. Chowdhury recalled Pelé’s pass drawing him away before Carlos Alberto arrived to score. Bagan goalkeeper Shibaji Banerjee also saved a Pelé free kick. The Brazilian did not find the net himself.

That matters because the match can disappear into the familiar story of a famous visitor and an adoring crowd. The men in green and maroon had their own work to do. Habib and Thapa scored against the touring champions; Banerjee and the defenders preserved the draw. Sarkar later recalled Pelé recognising him at the post-match gathering as the player wearing number 14 who had given him so little room.

Pelé’s presence made the day possible, and his impending retirement gave it the air of a farewell visit. What survives for Mohun Bagan is more precise than the fact that they shared a field with him: against the Cosmos and their international cast, they held their own.

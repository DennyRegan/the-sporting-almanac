The United States had gone 51 matches without defeat when they met Brazil in the 2007 Women’s World Cup semi-final. They had won the competition twice; Brazil had never reached the final. By the end of 27 September in Hangzhou, those records offered little comfort to the Americans. Brazil won 4–0, and Marta supplied the performance by which the match is still remembered.

Much of the pre-match discussion concerned an American selection. Coach Greg Ryan had replaced Hope Solo, his goalkeeper for the first four games, with Briana Scurry, the veteran of earlier US successes. It was a decision that would invite fierce argument after the match. It also risked obscuring what Brazil did to the players in front of her. Their passing and movement repeatedly carried them into dangerous positions, with Formiga giving Marta and Cristiane the platform to attack.

The lead came after 20 minutes. Formiga sent in a corner and Leslie Osborne, trying to head the ball clear, turned it into her own goal. Seven minutes later Marta took possession on the right, worked her way inside and struck a low left-footed shot past Scurry. The score was 2–0 before the US could establish any rhythm of their own.

Their task grew harder in first-half stoppage time when midfielder Shannon Boxx received a second yellow card. The decision was contentious, but it did not account for the gap in the sides’ attacking play. In the 56th minute Cristiane ran through and finished for 3–0. Brazil were heading for a final; Marta still had one goal left to score.

With 11 minutes remaining, she received the ball near the left edge of the penalty area, her back to goal and a defender close behind. A backheel sent Tina Ellertson the wrong way. Marta carried the ball into the area, changed direction again and finished with her right foot. It was a goal built from decisions made in fractions of a second, each one leaving another defender unable to intervene.

Marta later called it the best goal of her career. Her two goals that night helped earn her the tournament’s Golden Boot and Golden Ball, though Brazil lost the final to Germany three days later. That defeat belongs in the story of the 2007 team. So does this semi-final: the night Brazil beat the established power and reached a World Cup final for the first time, with its finest player deciding the match on her own terms.

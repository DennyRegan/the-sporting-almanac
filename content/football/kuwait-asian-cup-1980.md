Kuwait had already seen what Korea Republic could do to them. The visitors won 3–0 in their 1980 Asian Cup group meeting, and the hosts won just one of their first three matches. When the two sides met again in the final on 30 September, the tournament’s earlier scoreline pointed in one direction. The final went the other way: Kuwait won 3–0 and became Asian champions for the first time.

It was a title that had seemed possible long before it seemed likely that month. Kuwait had reached the Asian Cup final in 1976, losing to Iran. Four years later they were hosts, but Iran had won the last three editions and arrived as favourites. Korea Republic had won the competition’s first two editions, in 1956 and 1960, and their group win over Kuwait was emphatic. The home side still had work to do merely to reach the semi-finals.

They found their way through with a 4–0 win over Qatar in the last group match, Faisal Al-Dakhil scoring twice. In the semi-final they met Iran and ended the holders’ pursuit of a fourth consecutive title. Jasem Yaqoub and Al-Dakhil scored in a 2–1 victory. Korea Republic had topped Kuwait’s group and reached the final by beating DPR Korea with a late winner. The hosts had beaten the dominant side in the competition; now they had to face the side that had beaten them.

At the Sabah Al-Salem Stadium in Kuwait City, before a crowd of 25,000, Sa’ad Al-Houti gave the hosts an eighth-minute lead. Al-Dakhil made it 2–0 in the 34th minute and scored again in the 68th. There was no late reprieve for Korea Republic. A 3–0 defeat in the group stage had become a 3–0 victory in the final, the same numbers carrying an entirely different meaning.

The route matters as much as the reversal. Kuwait had needed to recover from a poor start, then beat the reigning champions and the team that had exposed them. Al-Dakhil’s two goals in the final continued the scoring that had rescued their group campaign. The trophy was earned over those successive tests, rather than through one surprise performance at the end.

Kuwait’s 1980 title became the continental achievement of a generation that would qualify for the 1982 World Cup in Spain. On 30 September, against an opponent with a recent 3–0 win over them, Kuwait returned the same score when a trophy was at stake.

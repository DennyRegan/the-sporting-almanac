Cameroon’s footballers had made a habit of surviving matches they seemed to have lost. They beat Brazil in the Sydney Olympic quarter-finals with a golden goal after being reduced to nine men. In the semi-final they trailed Chile late, then scored twice to reach the final. On 30 September 2000, in front of more than 100,000 people at Stadium Australia, Spain gave them their hardest recovery yet.

Xavi put Spain ahead with a free-kick in the second minute. Almost immediately, Cameroon goalkeeper Carlos Kameni, only 16, saved a penalty from Miguel Ángel Angulo, keeping the deficit at one. But Gabri scored shortly before half-time and Spain took a 2–0 lead into the dressing room. The Spanish side contained young players who would go on to much larger stages, including Xavi and Carles Puyol. Cameroon had Samuel Eto’o, Lauren, Geremi and Patrick Mboma. At the interval, the medals looked to be heading to Spain.

Cameroon’s reply began with an own goal by Iván Amaya early in the second half. Eto’o then equalised, completing a quick recovery to 2–2. In the space of a few minutes, Spain’s assurance had turned into a test of endurance. Gabri and José Mari were both sent off later in the match, leaving nine Spanish players to carry the game through extra time. There was no winner, and the final moved to penalties.

Cameroon took all five of theirs. Amaya, whose own goal had opened the way back, struck Spain’s only missed shoot-out penalty against the crossbar. The shoot-out finished 5–3. For Cameroon it was the country’s first Olympic gold medal. For Africa it was a second successive men’s football gold, following Nigeria’s victory in Atlanta four years earlier.

Mboma, one of the senior figures in the team, later recalled how low the mood had been at half-time. His argument to his team-mates was straightforward: Spain had scored twice in 45 minutes, so Cameroon could do the same. Kameni’s early save had kept the match alive, and Spain, even two men short, held out through extra time.

Cameroon had started their tournament with a 3–2 win over Kuwait and two draws. They ended it by taking five penalties without a miss. Between those points came the recoveries against Brazil, Chile and Spain. The last was the steepest: two goals down in an Olympic final, with the country’s first gold medal waiting on the other side.

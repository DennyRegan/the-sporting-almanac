Parimal Dey arrived late in the 1970 IFA Shield final. His goal arrived later still. On 25 September, the East Bengal substitute scored the only goal against PAS Tehran at Eden Gardens and gave his club one of the wins by which it still measures its history.

The visiting side had earned its place in the final with two emphatic results. PAS beat Bengal Nagpur Railway 5–0 in the quarter-final, then defeated Mohun Bagan 2–0. Seven goals scored and none conceded in those two matches made them formidable finalists. East Bengal’s route included a 5–0 win over Eastern Railway and a semi-final against Mohammedan Sporting. Its side included goalkeeper Peter Thangaraj, a member of India’s 1962 Asian Games winning squad, and defender Syed Nayeemuddin. By the time the Iranian club and the Calcutta club met, the result had significance beyond the Shield itself: PAS had already removed East Bengal’s great local rival.

Dey was an established forward, not an unknown waiting for a lucky moment. He had scored for East Bengal in the 1966 IFA Shield final against Bengal Nagpur Railway and had represented India. He would score 84 goals for East Bengal across his club career and had captained it in 1968. Yet his place in the 1970 final was close to being missed. In the club’s later tribute to captain Santo Mitra, East Bengal credited Mitra with insisting that Dey be included in the match squad shortly before kick-off. Dey eventually replaced Mohammed Habib and struck near the end.

Dey’s impact was immediate. The All India Football Federation says his goal remains the quickest scored by a substitute in the competition’s history. In a final that had remained goalless until its closing stages, East Bengal had found the answer in an experienced forward who had begun the game on the bench. PAS, after decisive wins earlier in the week, had no time to respond.

East Bengal had been founded in 1920, so this was its 50th year. It would also win the Calcutta Football League, completing a league-and-Shield double. For Dey, that was the second of three such doubles with the club. In the IFA Shield’s post-independence history, no Indian side had previously won the final against foreign opposition. East Bengal earned that distinction against an opponent that had just beaten Mohun Bagan.

The Shield returned to East Bengal in its jubilee year. PAS had arrived at the final after putting seven goals past its quarter-final and semi-final opponents without reply. Its only concession of the closing three matches came from a player East Bengal nearly left out. Santo Mitra’s selection call and Dey’s late finish had become inseparable.

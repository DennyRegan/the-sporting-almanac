Chile had been minutes from an Olympic final. Against Cameroon in the Sydney semi-final, Nelson Acosta’s side led 1–0 late in the game, only to concede twice and lose 2–1. Three days later, on 29 September 2000, they had to play again. The prize was bronze, which would still be Chile’s first medal in Olympic football, but the disappointment of the missed final could hardly be packed away so neatly.

Iván Zamorano gave them a way forward. The captain was one of the experienced players in a young squad, alongside a midfield that included David Pizarro and Claudio Maldonado and a forward partner in Reinaldo Navia. Chile had beaten Morocco and Spain in the group stage and Nigeria in the quarter-finals. The route had shown they could score freely; the loss to Cameroon had shown how quickly a tournament could turn.

The United States made the bronze-medal match a contest. Neither side scored in the first half at the Sydney Football Stadium, and Chile needed patience as much as urgency. Pizarro began to find room to send longer passes into the American defence. From one of those deliveries, substitute Sebastián González was fouled in the area. Zamorano took the penalty and scored in the 69th minute.

The opening goal changed the match. The US needed to advance, giving Chile room to break. In the 84th minute, a move involving Maldonado and substitute Rodrigo Núñez put Zamorano in position to score again. This was no ornamental second goal: it removed the possibility that the Americans might steal an equaliser and ensured that the team would leave Australia with a medal.

It also gave Zamorano six goals for the tournament, enough to finish as its leading scorer. His contribution had been spread across the fortnight, including three against Morocco, but the two in the last match gave his campaign its ending. Chile won 2–0. The following day Cameroon, the side that had taken their place in the final, came from two goals down to win gold against Spain.

Bronze can sound like a consolation when a semi-final has been lost so late. For Chile, this one marked ground the national team had not reached before. Zamorano did not erase the previous defeat; he made sure it was not the last act of Chile’s tournament. The captain’s final two goals turned a difficult return to the pitch into a place on the podium.

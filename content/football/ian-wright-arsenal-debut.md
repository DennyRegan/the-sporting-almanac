Arsenal signed Ian Wright from Crystal Palace on 23 September 1991. Two days later he scored for them at Leicester City. The League Cup first leg finished 1–1 at Filbert Street, but the new striker had given George Graham something immediate from his club-record £2.5 million signing.

Wright’s arrival was no speculative punt on an unknown. Palace had finished third in the First Division the previous season, with Wright and Mark Bright as the forward partnership in Steve Coppell’s side. Wright had also scored twice in Palace’s win over Everton in the Full Members’ Cup final. Coppell later recalled that, even after the club’s best league finish, the striker wanted to move to fulfil his ambitions. Palace knew what it was losing.

Arsenal’s bet carried its own question. Wright had reached professional football by a route that seldom produced a record signing. He played Sunday league while working as a plasterer and received his first professional contract at Palace only shortly before his 22nd birthday. By 1991 he had proved himself in the top division. Now he had to fit a side that had won the league that spring, losing just one match along the way.

Wright scored once at Leicester and the first leg ended level. It was a useful goal away from home in a two-legged tie, and the first occasion on which the club’s new centre-forward put his name on their scoresheet. The return at Highbury came in October; Wright scored again as Arsenal won 2–0 and advanced.

Alan Smith saw the debut goal from the stand. Injured that evening, Arsenal’s established striker had expected Wright’s pace to work alongside his own role as a target man. Years later, Smith wrote that Wright’s instinctive movement altered the team’s rehearsed patterns and unsettled his own game. The new arrival was changing more than the scoresheet.

Between those League Cup meetings came the display that made the Leicester goal seem like an opening note. On 28 September, Wright played his first league match for Arsenal at Southampton and scored a hat-trick in a 4–0 victory. The sequence was unusually compressed: transfer on Monday, debut goal on Wednesday, three more on Saturday. Supporters scarcely had time to learn the new signing’s habits before they were watching him celebrate repeatedly.

Wright went on to lead Arsenal’s scoring for six successive seasons and passed Cliff Bastin’s club record in 1997. Those numbers can make the beginning look inevitable. It was not. Palace had just lost a forward central to its finest season; Arsenal had paid a fee no player had cost them before. At Filbert Street, in a cup tie that remained undecided, Wright began answering both clubs’ questions in the way he would answer so many more: by scoring.

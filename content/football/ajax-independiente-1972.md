Ajax had won two successive European Cups when they faced Independiente in September 1972. Their football, developed under Rinus Michels and given more freedom by his successor Stefan Kovács, was admired far beyond Amsterdam. The Intercontinental Cup offered a different test: two matches against the South American champions, the first in Argentina and the second at home. Ajax had declined to play for the trophy after their first European Cup win in 1971. This time they took the trip.

The opening leg in Avellaneda ended 1–1. Johan Cruyff scored early, but Francisco Sá equalised late for Independiente. Nothing had been settled. On 28 September the teams met again at Amsterdam’s Olympic Stadium, with Ajax needing to turn their advantage of home ground into something more substantial than reputation.

Johan Neeskens supplied the first goal, arriving to finish in the 12th minute. That gave Ajax the lead in the match and across the tie, but Independiente remained within one goal of restoring parity. The Argentine side had won the Copa Libertadores that year and would win it again in the next three. This was no ceremonial appearance by the visitors, however imposing Ajax’s European record looked. Cruyff had scored both goals in the European Cup final against Inter Milan four months earlier; the Intercontinental Cup still needed to be won on its own terms.

The decisive change came from Ajax’s bench. Johnny Rep replaced Sjaak Swart in the second half and scored twice, with Cruyff involved in the making of both goals. Rep’s first took the score to 2–0 on the night; his second put the contest beyond Independiente. Ajax won 3–0, 4–1 on aggregate, and claimed the Intercontinental Cup for the first time.

Rep’s cameo is one of the details that keeps the result from becoming a simple roll call of famous names. Cruyff was the star, Neeskens the scorer who broke the deadlock, but a substitute finished the work. Ajax had enough invention in the side to alter a final without changing its principles. Their movement still made room; the new man took it.

The win placed a world club title beside the domestic league, Dutch Cup and European Cup that Kovács’s team had won earlier in 1972. Ajax would add a third consecutive European Cup in 1973. On this September night, facing the champions of South America, they made the strongest available case for the reach of that extraordinary side. The 1–1 in Avellaneda had left room for doubt. Rep’s two goals removed it.

Wayne Rooney was a Manchester United player before he was a Manchester United footballer. He had joined from Everton in the summer of 2004, but a broken foot had kept him out for three months. The fee and the performances he had given England at that year’s European Championship had raised expectations. On 28 September, against Fenerbahçe in the Champions League, he finally got a chance to answer them on the pitch.

It took United eight minutes to go ahead, Ryan Giggs heading in from Kléberson’s cross. Rooney had already tried to drive at the Turkish defence, and soon lifted a shot over after escaping his marker and goalkeeper Rüştü Reçber. It was an imperfect start, which made what followed feel like football rather than a scripted unveiling.

In the 17th minute Ruud van Nistelrooij found him outside the area. Rooney struck firmly into the net. Eleven minutes later, after Fenerbahçe had a goal disallowed, he shaped his way past a defender and shot from distance again. Two goals on debut, both taken early and with the conviction that had made him such a costly signing at 18.

Fenerbahçe refused to accept their place as scenery. Márcio Nobre scored soon after half-time, cutting United’s lead to 3–1. Rooney’s reply came from a free-kick on the edge of the area. He took it himself, lifting the ball over the wall for his first senior competitive hat-trick. There was still time for Tuncay Şanlı to score for the visitors, and for Van Nistelrooij and David Bellion to complete a 6–2 win. Rooney made Bellion’s goal as well, heading a clearance into his path.

The three finishes are usually shown together now: the force of the first, the quick movement before the second, the precision of the third. Watching them as a single sequence can hide the match around them. United had been held by Lyon in their opening group game. Fenerbahçe had dangerous players of their own, including Alex and Pierre van Hooijdonk. Rooney was entering a side that needed a European win, after weeks spent watching from the treatment room.

One evening cannot prove how a career will unfold. This one did establish that Rooney could carry the pressure attached to his move without waiting for a gentler introduction. He had arrived as a celebrated prospect. By the final whistle at Old Trafford, he had given United a result and its supporters a first clear sight of the player they had bought.
